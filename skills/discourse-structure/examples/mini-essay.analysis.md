# Structural Reconstruction

## Global Architecture

The essay moves from a problem of apparent disagreement, to a conceptual distinction that diagnoses the problem, to a methodological conclusion about the order of good argument.

## Hierarchy

- **Work:** *Why Definitions Matter in Disagreement* — authorial title
  - **Inferred §1:** Problem: apparent disagreement may conceal different questions
  - **Inferred §2:** Distinction: verbal versus substantive disagreement
  - **Inferred §3:** Consequence: clarification can precede evaluation of reasons

## Major Logical Dependencies

1. `sec-1 --requires--> sec-2`  
   The distinction is introduced as a response to the problem framed in the opening.  
   Evidence: paragraphs 1–2. Confidence: 0.90.

2. `sec-2 --requires--> sec-3`  
   The methodological conclusion presupposes the distinction established immediately before it.  
   Evidence: paragraphs 2–3. Confidence: 0.96.

3. `sec-2 --supports--> sec-3`  
   The distinction supplies the reason why clarification can have priority in some disputes.  
   Evidence: paragraphs 2–3. Confidence: 0.87.

## Cross-Hierarchy Relations

None in this short example.

## Uncertain or Alternative Readings

The relation between §1 and §2 could be represented as `sequence` if one interprets §2 as merely an explanatory continuation rather than a prerequisite. The stronger `requires` reading is retained because §2 is explicitly introduced as the diagnostic tool needed by the problem in §1.
