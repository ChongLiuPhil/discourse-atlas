# Analysis Principles

## Summary versus structural role

Always distinguish:

- **content:** what a unit says;
- **function:** what kind of discourse move it performs;
- **role in parent:** why the parent needs this unit;
- **dependency:** what other unit(s) it relies on or affects.

A useful reconstruction answers not only "What is §3 about?" but "Why does §3 need to occur here, and what later work depends on it?"

## Local and global structure

A text can have a clean local sequence but complex global dependencies. Preserve both.

## Alternative readings

When two reconstructions are genuinely plausible, record the stronger one in the graph only if defensible and mention the alternative in `analysis.md`. If neither is sufficiently supported, omit the edge.

## Avoid overformalization

Not every rhetorical transition is an inference. Not every example supports a thesis. Not every definition is required by every later occurrence of the term. The graph should clarify the text, not create false precision.
